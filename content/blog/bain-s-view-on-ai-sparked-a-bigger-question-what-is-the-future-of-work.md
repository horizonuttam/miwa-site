_Future of Work_

I recently read Bain & Company's article, _"How Will AI Change Software Organizations?"_, and while the discussion is framed around software engineering, I couldn't help but think that its core message extends far beyond technology.

Bain argues that AI is not simply making developers more productive; it is changing how organizations are structured, how teams collaborate, and where human value is created. That perspective resonates with several other recent studies. McKinsey has similarly argued that becoming "AI-centric" requires organizations to rethink their operating models rather than simply adopting new tools, while Microsoft's **2026 Work Trend Index** introduces the idea that as AI agents take on execution, humans gain greater agency to direct work, make decisions, and own outcomes. Across these independent perspectives, a common pattern is emerging: the real disruption is not AI itself, but the redesign of work around AI.

What makes this shift fascinating is that it is not confined to software development. Every knowledge-intensive profession is beginning to experience the same transition. Engineers are increasingly reviewing and orchestrating AI-generated code instead of writing every line themselves. Consultants are spending less time building slide decks and more time solving client problems. Researchers are using AI to synthesize literature and analyze large datasets before applying their own expertise to validate findings. Doctors are interpreting AI-assisted diagnoses, lawyers are reviewing AI-generated contracts, educators are personalizing learning experiences with AI, and manufacturers are integrating intelligent systems into production planning. The common thread across all these professions is that AI is steadily taking over execution, allowing humans to focus on context, judgment, creativity, ethics, and decision-making- the capabilities that remain difficult to automate. In many ways, the future of work is less about becoming better at doing tasks and more about becoming better at orchestrating and architecting intelligent systems.

This is why I believe organizations should stop asking, _"How can AI improve productivity?"_ and start asking, _"If AI can execute much of today's work, how should we redesign work itself?"_ History shows that transformative technologies rarely create value by replacing people; they create value by changing how people work together.

AI appears to be following the same path. The organizations that thrive won't necessarily be those with access to the most advanced models, but those that redesign their workflows, invest in AI literacy, and empower people to work alongside intelligent systems. The future of work is unlikely to be a story of humans versus AI. It will be a story of organizations that successfully combine human judgment with machine intelligence to solve problems that neither could solve as effectively alone.
